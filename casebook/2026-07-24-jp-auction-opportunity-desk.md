## Summary
The product needed a calm local desk for scouting previously untracked, ending-soon auction opportunities. A rectangular auction paddle won because it names the domain without a gavel, while one large hourglass cut carries the verified-deadline job at 16 px.

## What failed first
- The lot-stub finalist's side notches disappeared below 24 px, so the intended
  physical ticket silhouette collapsed into a generic hourglass-on-card.
- The sandglass-token finalist stayed legible, but the name-the-thing answer was
  only “timer”; its auction meaning depended on a punched hole that became
  secondary at 16 px.
- The winner reversed that hierarchy: the broad rectangular head and two-pixel
  handle make the auction paddle the first reading, while the deadline remains
  one 136-unit negative-space cut. Its first master review cleared every axis.

## Lessons
- [x] When a deadline tool risks generic clock or gavel symbols, use a
  domain-specific object silhouette and carry time as one two-pixel-safe
  negative-space cut. This is already covered by L1, L2, L15, L20, and the
  Concepting specificity gate.
