## Summary
The catalog expanded from fourteen to twenty technique grammars without treating
palette, stroke weight, or large-size decoration as new styles. Cel shading,
chrome, ink brush, woodcut, glass stacking, and weaving survived the shared-rail
gallery, native 16 px proof, target review, and nearest-family comparison.

## What failed first
The first proposed `plush` family repeated the same layered rounded-stroke model
as `clay` and `cut-paper`; its defining seam collapsed into an ordinary highlight
at 16 px, leaving distinctiveness at 3. It was removed rather than cosmetically
polished and replaced with a closed woodcut relief mass whose 128-unit edge-open
gouges remained structurally separate from both stencil holes and ink-brush
pressure gestures. Early glass panes also repeated the rail three times and read
as a glitch at 16 px; two broad panes around one opaque skeleton restored
legibility. Chrome reflections became three explicit hard bands, while ink-brush
cuts, woven gaps, and cel contours were brought onto deliberate pixel budgets.
