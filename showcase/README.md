# IconFlow Theme Worlds

Theme Worlds prove the second half of IconFlow's range: a technique is how an
icon is constructed; a world is what the icon is about and why somebody needs
it. Every published master in this directory passed the same 16 px and
source-bound review gate as the IconFlow brand.

The nine launch icons are original clean-room designs. Museum references were
used to study general construction, posture, and material history—not to trace
or reproduce an artwork.

## Launch briefs and divergence

| World | User job → essence | Four concept lenses | Cliché avoided | Winning device | Technique |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Keepsake Knot | maintain a shared ritual → `together` | ritual knot; shared doorway; repaired seam; passing baton | heart, chat bubble, linked circles | two unequal bands share one offset opening | woven |
| Catnap Focus | settle into focused rest → `settle` | curled body; warm nest; dimming eyelid; folded blanket | cat head, paw, clock | the tail leaves one broad time wedge | clay |
| Trail Tail | remember a dog's walking route → `roam` | side-view stride; leash loop; scent trail; collar clasp | paw, map pin, dog head | one contour becomes the route, with an off-axis clasp | line mark |
| Koi Return | sustain a daily habit → `return` | upstream fish; turning pond; scale ledger; wake gate | yin-yang fish, waves, aquarium bowl | a side-view koi owns one edge-open upstream cut | stained glass |
| Quiet Hero | express an original persona state → `poise` | profile bust; high collar; hair crest; quiet visor | generic chibi, animal ears, franchise imitation | asymmetric profile crest meets one high collar notch | cel shaded |
| Boss Helm | record a team raid achievement → `conquer` | broken horn; lowered visor; raid scar; arena gate | trophy, crown, skull, sword, gamepad | one broken horn makes the frontal silhouette ownable | chrome |
| Sky Courier | follow an original serial without spoilers → `anticipate` | winged satchel; courier swallow; sealed air letter; horizon relay | star, wand, play button, calendar | a satchel flap becomes the wing and owns one edge-open dry cut | ink brush |
| Co-op Lock | coordinate lobby readiness → `sync` | unequal keycaps; docking cards; shared counter; split lobby gate | controller, check mark, two avatars | two unequal keycaps lock around one shared opaque counter | glass stack |
| Forest Familiar | guide a small companion home → `return` | perched trail owl; pine-marten path; lantern moth; carved nest gate | generic animal head, fox repeat, tree badge | one ear tuft and two edge-open wing gouges define a full-body owl | woodcut |

Each brief produced two vector finalists in `work/theme-worlds/`. The promoted
master, review receipt, and deterministic web build remain beside each brief;
the website publishes the exact approved SVG plus its deterministic native
16 px render rather than a regenerated mockup.

## Research provenance

- The Metropolitan Museum of Art, *Textile Fragment with Interlace Pattern*
  and *Tunic with Interlace Decoration* (public domain): interlace is treated as
  over/under topology with visible crossings, not surface decoration.
- The Metropolitan Museum of Art, Japanese dog netsuke and dog-with-leash
  netsuke (public domain): compact side posture and the leash as a semantic
  relationship were studied; no historical figure was copied.
- The Art Institute of Chicago public-domain Japanese collection and carp
  conservation notes: side-view carp anatomy and fin hierarchy informed the
  koi noun test; the pane geometry is original.
- The Metropolitan Museum of Art helmet catalog (*Kawari Kabuto*,
  *Suji-Kabuto*, and *Zukinnari Kabuto*): helmet mass, crest placement, and
  protective overlap were studied; Boss Helm is an original fantasy object,
  not a reconstruction of Japanese armor.
- The Museum of Modern Art and The Metropolitan Museum of Art woodcut process
  notes: Forest Familiar follows the general raised-field/carved-recess logic;
  its owl geometry and gouges are original and no artwork or path was copied.
- Sky Courier and Co-op Lock are clean-room product metaphors. Ink pressure,
  pane overlap, and material hierarchy were studied as construction behavior,
  not by imitating a named artist, studio, franchise, or product mark.
